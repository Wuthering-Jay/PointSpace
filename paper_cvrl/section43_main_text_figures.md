## Fig. 5

![Fig. 5 - Visible-Surface Bottleneck](results/section_43/figure_5_visibility_bottleneck.png)

**建议图注：** Fig. 5. Geometry-only visibility grouping across Swiss-Audit. Vertical 100%-stacked bars show the proportions of high-confidence invisible (`P_inv`), ambiguous (`P_amb`), and visible (`P_vis`) points by (a) six mutually exclusive tile strata, (b) every LAS class present except Unclassified, and (c) height above local ground. Across all 95,965,298 audit points, the respective proportions are 19.73%, 13.53%, and 66.74%. The six scene strata cover all 406 tiles and are descriptive rather than manual land-use labels: building-rich tiles have ≥15% roof points and are divided by roof-point 90th-percentile height above ground at 15 m; dense-canopy tiles have ≥60% vegetation; flat bare ground requires ≥75% ground, <5% vegetation, <5% roof, and ≤5 m ground-elevation 2nd–98th-percentile relief; sparse vegetation has 15–60% vegetation and <75% ground; all remaining tiles are mixed/other. Strata are assigned in the order above. The strict flat-bare-ground stratum contains only three tiles and should be interpreted descriptively, not as a region-wide prevalence estimate. Classes 26 and 27 occur in the second Swiss acquisition cycle and must not be read as cross-cycle prevalences. `q_geo` is a geometry-only observability proxy, not pointwise optical visibility ground truth.

## Fig. 6

![Fig. 6 - Projection conflict mechanism](results/section_43/figure_6_projection_conflict_mechanism.png)

**建议图注：** Fig. 6. From geometric occlusion to two-dimensional projection mismatch. (a) LAS-label patch-class conflict rates for `P_vis`, `P_amb`, and `P_inv`; error bars denote paired source-block bootstrap 95% confidence intervals from the original Swiss-Audit analysis. Among 1,405,737 eligible audit points, `P_inv` exceeds `P_vis` by 24.35 percentage points (95% CI: 22.74–25.98). Eligible patches have at least five valid visible-point correspondences and at least 70% majority-class purity. (b) Under-canopy ground and (c) building facade are illustrative 200 × 200 m Swiss tiles, not members of the 100 m quantitative audit. The four equally spaced columns show an orthophoto, an aligned equal-scale square-projection ALS top view, a LAS-class-colored elevation profile, and a visibility-colored elevation profile. In each orthophoto, the red ring locates an actual 16 × 16 px DINO correspondence patch; an arrow points from the ring to the local magnification, which marks a sample of projected `P_inv` target ALS points in red. The matching red ring in the ALS top view marks their planimetric location. The modal LAS class among valid surface correspondences is Vegetation for the under-canopy Ground example (54 surface points; 100% purity; 165 `P_inv` Ground points projecting into the patch), and Building roof for the Facade example (60 surface points; 98% purity; 157 `P_inv` Facade points). Thus the annotations explicitly connect the statistical conflict in (a) to the two physical projection mechanisms. The two profiles use exactly the same points from a 0.5 m-thick section, identical axes, and unmodified LAS elevation; their colors are deliberately separated rather than blended. In the rightmost column, blue, gold, and red correspond to `P_vis`, `P_amb`, and `P_inv`, matching panel (a). For these *qualitative examples only*, the shared multi-offset Z-buffer estimator uses a 0.18 m geometric splat radius to reduce canopy-gap false visibility; the Fig. 6(a) rates and Fig. 5 statistics retain their original automatic-radius setting and are **not directly pooled with or recalculated from the examples**. Neither case visibility calculation uses return number or LAS class as input. The top-down point cloud uses PCV, edge-only EDL, and adaptive circular point splats (`scale=0.86`). The same dashed **A–A′** line in the first two columns marks the section used by both profiles. No visibility colors are overlaid on the orthophoto, ALS top view, or class profile. The conflict measure uses post-hoc LAS labels as a visible-surface proxy; **it is not a DINO prediction error rate**. The two spatial cases illustrate mechanisms rather than population frequencies.
