# Geometric observability method figure

## Figure caption

**Geometric observability and visual supervision partition in a real ALS scene.** (a) Geometric observability in an ALS profile: a real Swiss-Audit profile is colored by the geometry-only score \(q_i^{geo}\); the light gray curve is a local orthographic upper-surface envelope estimated from the complete point cloud. Three real returns are highlighted without formula annotations. (b) Spatial observability and supervision partition: the same tile is rendered from an oblique orthographic viewpoint with physical-height occlusion. The projected XYZ bounding cuboid has pale-gray floor and rear walls, sparse three-face grids, local X/Y metre ticks, and Z elevation ticks in metres. The blue translucent plane labeled “Profile A–A′” corresponds to (a); its dashed outline is hidden where actual points pass in front. For legibility, the displayed plane extends beyond the exact profile endpoints and elevation limits, but this does not change the profile point selection. The color-scale thresholds \(\tau_{low}=0.20\) and \(\tau_{high}=0.60\) divide points into \(\mathcal{P}_{inv}\), \(\mathcal{P}_{amb}\), and \(\mathcal{P}_{vis}\). Only \(\mathcal{P}_{vis}\) is eligible for direct VPSD supervision when a valid image correspondence exists.

## Reproduction and interpretation

Run `D:/app/anaconda3/envs/pointspace/python.exe paper_cvrl/plot_geometric_observability_method.py` from the repository root. The script reads the original full-resolution LAS and its precomputed full-resolution `q_geo` sidecar. The profile, envelope, partition, and oblique rendering all use the complete tile; no display sampling is currently needed.

The displayed envelope is a local max-Z visualization sampled every 0.4 m along the transect within a 1.1 m horizontal radius. Its light smoothing affects only the drawn line. The three highlighted points have measured `q_geo` values recorded in the JSON provenance file; their mathematical callouts and leader lines are deliberately omitted for later manual annotation. The actual `q_geo` is the existing four-offset adaptive-splat geometry estimator; the envelope is an explanatory geometric reference, not an alternate estimator. No LAS class, return attribute, DINO feature, or semantic prediction is used in computing the score or rendering the figure. The tile's real class inventory was checked only to select a mixed urban and vegetation scene.

The continuous display palette runs from saturated blue at low `q_geo` through vivid cyan/gold to vermilion at high `q_geo`. The oblique view uses the project's density-adaptive circular point renderer on all tile points, with fractional splats and tiny-gap filling. Overlap follows physical camera depth, rather than artificially prioritizing low scores. This preserves truthful roof and canopy occlusion while exposing side and interior returns through the lower, 32° elevation viewpoint. The flat 2D frame and former A–A′ dashed line have been removed. The XYZ cuboid is drawn behind rasterized points; its light-gray floor and rear walls distinguish the 3D volume from the white page, with dashed metric grids on all three planes. The black `X (m)`, `Y (m)`, and `Z (m)` labels and numeric ticks sit beyond the outer edges; X/Y numbers are relative to the tile minimum coordinates, while Z is absolute elevation. The section plane is not a translucent rectangle pasted over the rendered image: its camera depth is compared with the point renderer's depth buffer per pixel. Points in front retain their original color and occlude the plane; points behind show through its blue translucency. The black dashed outline is similarly depth-tested. The display-only plane extends 8 m past each end of the transect and 3 m below / 5 m above its data elevation range. The original transect and measured `q_geo` remain unchanged. The colorbar keeps the numeric thresholds but omits formula labels for later manual annotation. The exported PNG is tightly cropped.

The JSON file of the same stem records the source paths, transect, parameters, thresholds, point counts, and exact indices and scores of the three marked ALS points.
