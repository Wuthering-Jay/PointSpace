# Fig. 9 · Vertical cross-section transfer comparison

![Fig. 9](figure_9_vertical_transfer.png)

**Suggested caption (English).** Fig. 9. Targeted vertical cross-section comparisons under frozen-encoder decoder probing. Columns show ground truth, Scratch, Sonata, D-DITR, VPSD, and CVRL predictions for identical LiDAR points. The horizontal axis is distance along each approximately 50 m profile, in metres; the vertical axis is absolute elevation, in metres. DALES and Vaihingen use 0.50 m-wide slabs; OpenGF uses a 0.75 m-wide slab. Profiles are scattered directly from original XYZ coordinates at equal horizontal and vertical metric scale, with no intermediate raster resizing. No error boxes or other interpretive annotations are drawn. These are intentionally selected positive qualitative cases, not unbiased estimates of error frequency. The red horizontal structure in the DALES row has LAS truck labels, not building labels; the plotted color and legend are consistent with those labels.

For DALES and Vaihingen, candidate selection used the seven GT-screened Fig. 8 tiles per dataset. For OpenGF, a separate [varied-structure screen](qualitative_cache/opengf_urban_profile_scan.json) examined all 1,059 test tiles using GT-only geometry, then evaluated 50 m sections in 693 tiles with 20–85% non-ground points and at least 6 m tile relief. Both horizontal directions, 19 cross-track positions, and seven 50 m longitudinal windows were examined. The section had to contain at least two 5 m bins in which ground and non-ground coexist with at least 2 m median vertical separation. Eligible sections were rechecked with all five models. The displayed section has 239 labelled points and a CVRL point-accuracy gain of at least eight percentage points over **every** plotted baseline. It maximizes gain over the strongest baseline times the square root of point count within that eligible set. The complete Fig. 9 section audit is in [`qualitative_cache/vertical_selection_audit.json`](qualitative_cache/vertical_selection_audit.json). This is a deliberately targeted local case, not an unbiased sample.

The OpenGF section contains multiple elevated non-ground surfaces and lower ground, with about 13.5 m local relief and 70.7% non-ground points; it is no longer a single hillside. Locally CVRL accuracy is 0.9958 versus 0.8870 for D-DITR, 0.8619 for Scratch, 0.8033 for VPSD, and 0.7238 for Sonata. The most visible difference is erroneous non-ground prediction on the lower ground segment in the Sonata and VPSD columns, which CVRL does not show. On the complete `T2_0004.las` tile, CVRL mIoU is 0.8504 versus 0.6340 for VPSD, 0.6321 for D-DITR, 0.5905 for Scratch, and 0.5668 for Sonata. Nevertheless, this tile and section were selected for a positive qualitative example and do not estimate whole-test-set performance. OpenGF has only ground/non-ground labels, so the figure cannot by itself establish whether the elevated surfaces are buildings. All panels within a row use the same points, metric axis limits, point size, and class colors. The Vaihingen car color is teal, distinct from the pink-red roof class. No coordinates, labels, or predictions were edited.

The GT-first structural screen yielded 49 sections meeting the spatial-overlap and three-model gain criteria. Eight tile-distinct leading candidates were subsequently checked against Scratch and D-DITR as well; the final row's minimum gain over all four plotted baselines is 10.88 percentage points. This selection is outcome-aware and should be disclosed as such in the caption or text.
